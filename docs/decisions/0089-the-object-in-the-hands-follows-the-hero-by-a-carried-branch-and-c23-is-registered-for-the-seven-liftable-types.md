# ADR-0089: The object in the hands follows the hero by a carried branch of the motion, the three modes of UpdatePlayerCarriedEntity are pure rules, and C23 is registered for the seven liftable types

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-24
- **Source**: this chantier: `docs/plan-e20-heros.md` section 1.24.1 (E20.g1a, D-G-2, D-G-3, D-G-4, D-G-5, D-G-9, D-G-11, D-G-12, D-G-13, D-G-15, D-G-17) and the block "Revision au HEAD (2026-10-18)"; reading of `ALUN_CD.EXE` (France) in `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g1a/` (`UpdatePlayerCarriedEntity` `0x8002F884`, `StepTowards` `0x8002F854`, the carried branch of `MoveEntity` `0x80037E34`, the zeroing of the forces `0x80036A84`, the carrying group `0x8003232C`, the lift gestures `0x800322F0` and `0x800322B4`, the program C23 `0x8006B848`, the clearing of the carrier at the end of the destructions `0x8003A62C`, `0x8003A74C` and `0x8003A7A4`, the sort driver `0x800397AC`); measured results in `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g1a/exec/results.md`

## Context

- Before this slice nothing in the DLL put an object in the hands of the hero: `UpdatePlayerCarriedEntity` was a named stub, the states 0x05, 0x06, 0x07, 0x0B, 0x0C and 0x2E to 0x30 had no case, `PlatformEntity` and `CarriedEntity` were never written, the two overloads of `WorldProxy.DestroyEntity` left the clearing of the carrier as a named stub, and no native program was registered for the crates, jars and stones (the seven sprite types whose header holds C23 in the export).
- In the binary an object in the hands has no motion of its own: its `+0x28` names the carrier, the motion pass gives it the carrier's position plus the offset the carrier holds (`+0x30/+0x34/+0x38`), its forces are zeroed and its heights are the carrier's. The offset is drawn in by `UpdatePlayerCarriedEntity` (mode 0 on every free tick, 1 pixel a tick; mode 1 in the locked branch, 2 pixels a call after five calls, and a destruction when the object is within `0x1FFFF` on the three axes; mode 2 for a wound, deep water or a death, which lets the object go with the code of throw -1).
- The trigger (lifting), the throw and the breaking are other slices (E20.g1b, g2a, g2b); this slice must leave production inert: nothing but the new seam puts an object in the hands.

## Decision

- One internal seam puts an object in the hands: `PlayerManager.PutInHands(hero, item)` (D-G-2). It writes the two links as logic entities (`CarriedEntity` of the hero, `PlatformEntity` of the object; no retyping, D-G-15), the offset as the object's raw position minus the hero's, clears the object's `RidingEntity` (D-G-12), poses the marker of the logical Z on the object and never removes it (D-G-13), and gives the hero the plain carrying state when the object stood on his head, otherwise the lift gesture, on the ground or in the air. It has no production caller.
- The rules are pure functions in `CarryRules` (D-G-4, D-G-5): `StepTowards`, `FreeOffset` (mode 0; the disassembly branches on "less than", so equality moves the three axes), `LockedPull`, `CanDestroyLocked`, `UpdateCarried` (the three modes; any value other than 0 and 1 is mode 2; a sheet of balance without an attack turns mode 1 into mode 0; mode 1 destroys through the single-argument `IEntityWorldContext.DestroyEntity`, so with no loot and no effect), the table of the carrying group and the end of the two lift gestures. The lock counter is a field of the hero (`CarryLockCounter`), reset in modes 0 and 2 and when nothing is carried, kept after the destruction. The offsets are read on the positions, not on the offset words.
- The follow is a branch at the head of `EntityScriptProxy.TickMotion` for an active entity whose `PlatformEntity` has a proxy (D-G-3): no kinematic step and no support, forces and target forces to 0, `ForceAdjusted` and `CollidedWithEntityZ` to 1, `XCollisionEntity` none, `IsOnGround` 0, the floor and terrain heights copied from the carrier, the position set from the carrier and its offset, the modded position recomputed, the root pushed at a whole pixel. A `PlatformEntity` without a proxy (the bare entities of the oracle montages of E14) is no carrier: the entity moves as any other. The object leaves the list of the collidables through the existing `PlatformEntity == null` gate of `EntitySupport.IsEligibleSubject`.
- The hero's states are wired in the chain of `MovePlayer`: the lift gestures 0x05 and 0x0B and the carrying group 0x06, 0x07, 0x0C, 0x2E, 0x2F and 0x30 (the direction of the pad is written, then the item driver, then the group; kind 3 keeps the plain state; the ground properties `NoAttackVramFlag` and `NoJumpVramFlag` forbid the throw and the jump). The Square throw is a named stub at its exact place, replaced by E20.g2a. The jump is driven by the exported animation sheets (no jump code).
- Both overloads of `WorldProxy.DestroyEntity` end by clearing the carrier's `CarriedEntity` with no test of identity (D-G-11); the destroyed entity keeps its own `PlatformEntity`. A carrier without a proxy is left alone. The draw sort raises an object in the hands to at least the key of its carrier (`EntityDepthKey.RaiseToCarrier`, called from `FrameSyncPasses.RunWallInterleaveSortKeyPass`, which is where the sort keys of the entities are written; `EntityDepthKey` alone serves only the effect anchors).
- C23 is `LiftableAi` (D-G-9): nothing while not carried; carried without a code of throw, the animation 0 with the links kept; carried with a code (1, 2, 3, -1 through the table `0x80027400`), the animation of the flight, the code back to 0, the two links and the riding relation cleared, the flags `(f | 0x30) & ~0x80`. Phase C registers the key (C, 23) for the seven types (234, 291, 292, 294, 297, 304, 438) in `NativeSpriteRegistry.CreateForProduction`; the three pins of the key count of the registry are derived from the classes, not pasted (D-G-17). E2 stays to E20.g2b.
- The type 35 of the map 231 (record 3) is not added to the seven types: the export reads it as another sprite (O-G-11).

## Consequences

- A test (and E20.g1b later) can put a crate in the hands and see the lift series, the walk at the pace of the free walk, the jumps of the sheets, the wall and top tests identical to the free hero, a moving platform carrying hero and crate, the lock drawing the object in and destroying it at the 22nd call, the water letting it go, and a destroyed object freeing the hero's hands. With no production caller, production behaviour is unchanged: the 100-frame test of the crates at rest of the map 392 keeps every field but six clocks.
- The registry of production has one key more; a new class that registers must change the three pins by derivation.
- Known limits: the commun T0 of the group (oracle copy, probes P-G-1 to P-G-5) is owed to the slice and is not on the branch; the throw, the lifting, the breaking, the loot, the sites of the wound, the death and the exit square in `UpdatePlayerCarriedEntity` (named stubs where nothing reaches them) are other slices; the behaviour of the lock past the 21st call is inference, to be measured in g4b.
- The single-argument destruction is kept for mode 1 on purpose (no break effect, no loot). The mutant that routes it through the two-argument overload is killed only because the test double of the montage is not a `WorldProxy`; the "no effect" property of the real single-argument overload is existing E19 behaviour and has no test of its own here.
- Revert: phase C alone (one line in `CreateForProduction` and its pins), then phase B alone (the six branchings), then phase A (inert code and tests); no data or format change.

## Amendment (E20.g2a, 2026-10-24): the throw

- **Source**: `docs/plan-e20-heros.md` section 1.24.2 (E20.g2a, D-G-10, D-F3-2, D-F5-5) and the block "Revision au HEAD (2026-10-18)"; `ALUN_CD.EXE` (France), the Square branch of the carrying group `0x8003237C`-`0x80032404`; measured results in `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g2a/exec/README.md`.
- The Square branch of the carrying group, a named stub in g1a, calls `CarryRules.ThrowCarried(hero, ground, directionHeld)` (phase B, one call in `PlayerManager.CarryGroupTick`). The function is pure on the hero and the object in his hands and writes, in the binary's order: the direction word of the object from the table `0x80023644` (`PlayerManager.SpawnDirections`) indexed by the hero's `BoxDirection` (`+0x98`, the direction of the PREVIOUS tick: never the pad of the tick, which the group has just written to `TargetDirection`, nor the end-of-frame `AnimationDirection`; probe P-G-2 holds at 1, 2 and 3 ticks per frame); the object's X and Y from the hero and its Z from the hero's plus `0x200000`; on the ground the hero's state `0x09` and the code `+0x24` of the object 1 (no direction held) or 2, in the air `0x0A` and 2 or 3. It writes nothing when the ground forbids the throw (`CombinedVramFlagsOR & 0x2000`, `NoAttackVramFlag`). The links, the flags and the marker are left to C23, which turns the code into a projectile at its own tick in the same tick. `0x4000` (`NoJumpVramFlag`) forbids only the jump. The two lift gestures are not in the carrying group, so the Square never throws during them.
- The speed of the thrown object from the very tick of the throw needs no new line: the key of the kinematic tick of `ScriptedMotion.TickScriptedNpc` already reads `TargetAnimationId` for an entity with the marker of the logical Z (`HasLogicalZ`, written by the birth by the interface and by `PutInHands`), so the object moves 1.5 px at the tick T (X 110.5 against 109.0 without the clause; probe P-G-3). The retirement of that clause turns 47 tests red (the Carry, `MapStateTests` and `RecordBirthTickTests` families), `MapStateTests.P3_...` and `RecordBirthTickTests.B6_R_...` staying green.
- Without E2 (g2b) the landed object is not destroyed and keeps its height; its force is never cleared, so on the ground it glides on until a wall or the edge of the map (finding F2 of the annexe). One known gap, not fixed here: the object takes the hero's raw X and Y while its root is the whole-pixel position of the follow, so a hero stopped on a fraction of a pixel throws an object whose first step starts from the whole pixel (finding F1, a change of g1a's follow).
- Revert: the call of phase B alone (the stub back); phase A is inert code and tests.

## Amendment (E20.g2b, 2026-10-24): the break

- **Source**: `docs/plan-e20-heros.md` section 1.24.3 (E20.g2b, D-G-14) and the block "Revision au HEAD (2026-10-18)"; `ALUN_CD.EXE` (France), the program E2 `0x8007ED30` (model `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g1a/work/g1a_models.py`, function `e2`, 6 000 vectors without gap); measured results in `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g2b/exec/README.md`.
- E2 is `LiftableAi.Break`, run at every tick of the state Deactivated (slot E) of a thrown object. If the animation asked (`+0x88`, `TargetAnimationId`: the binary reads the animation ASKED, not the validated one) is 2 and the end flag (`+0xAC`) is 1, or the four corners stand on the ground property 4 (`+0x184 & 4`), the object is destroyed with `DestroyEntity(e, -1)` (the loot, the status 4, the break effect of its header, the carrier); otherwise it asks for the animation 2 and its flags become `(flags | 0x40) & ~0x30` (the crate `0x332374` becomes `0x332344`). An entity without a host destroys nothing.
- `LiftableAi.RegisterBreak` registers the key (E, 2) for the ten sprite types whose header holds E2 in the export: the seven of C23 plus the stone (290), the barrel (293) and the trunk (373) of the family C4. `BreakSpriteTypes` and `BreakKeys` are separate from the C23 lists so that phase A moved no pin; phase B (`NativeSpriteRegistry.CreateForProduction`) calls it, and the four pins of the production registry (`NativeSpriteRegistryTests`, `WaveSwordLifeTests`, `ChargedArrowAiTests`, `LiftableAiRegistrationTests`) are rewritten by derivation (the count before plus `LiftableAi.BreakKeys.Count`), not pasted.
- Measured chronology of a thrown crate on flat ground (the same at 1, 2 and 3 ticks per frame): arrival T+22, E2 first call T+23 (Deactivated, animation 2, sound 27), status 4 at T+25 with the break effect 10 (the jar, 2), slot free at T+26; a Beannoid touched at x = 165 breaks the object at T+22 (status 4 at T+24); deep water destroys with the effect 6 before E2 runs. An object at rest never raises the slot E (100 ticks on eight real maps: zero call). The animation end is validated at the end of the frame, so at 3 ticks per frame a first call on the first tick of its frame (the wall, the step of 16 px) shifts the destruction by one tick: the existing frame clock, not E2's code.
- The loot is the one of E20.e0c (`LootDrop`, called by `DestroyEntity(e, -1)`), derived from `ContentsItemId`; the damage the object deals to the enemies (E14) and the family C4 programs (E20.g2c) are other slices.
- Revert: the line of phase B in `CreateForProduction` and the four pins (the break is then inert in production); phase A is inert code and tests.

## Amendment (E20.g1b, 2026-10-24): the lift

- **Source**: `docs/plan-e20-heros.md` section 1.24.4 (E20.g1b, D-E20-71, D-E20-77, erratum of D-E20-69); `ALUN_CD.EXE` (France), `PlayerTryAction` `0x8002EEAC`, `TryLift` `0x8002EDBC`, the climb state `0x800321D4`-`0x800321E4`; `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g3/critic/report.md` (R1, R2, R4); measured results in `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g1b/README.md` and `t4/`.
- **The activation** is three lines changed together (`PlayerManager`): the body of `PlayerTryAction` calls the pure `PlayerTryActionCore` over the spawned entities of the world, the object `0x3B` (`GetNumberOfItem`) and the wired set; the climb states (0x0E, 0x35) call `TryUseItem` then `PlayerTryAction`, a non-zero answer ending the tick; `ProductionWiredLiftableTypes` is `LiftableAi.SpriteTypes`, the seven types of C23. Every other family (C4: barrel, stone, trunk, plate objects; the torch of the map 86; the bomb) stays outside the set and gives the sword.
- **The rules** (binary): the contact is `XCollisionEntity` of the PREVIOUS tick (the mover rewrites it at every step, nil when nothing blocked); with a rising edge of the Square, the answer of `TryLift` is returned directly, even 0 (no fall to the loop); without it, a loop with no button lifts an entity of status 2 or 3, not blocked (`+0x20` nil), whose `RidingEntity` (`+0x12C`) is this hero (a hero with no logic entity lifts nothing: nil must not equal nil). `TryLift`: genre 0 refuses, genre 1 lifts, genres 2 and 3 lift only with the object `0x3B`; no test of distance or gaze. The lift comes BEFORE `CheckEntityInteraction` in the order of the tick (a liftable that also holds an F program is lifted, the interaction never runs); PlayerTryAction is also called from the jump and air states, so a Square in flight lifts the object under the contact.
- **The map of the recipe is the 72** (D-E20-71), preset `porter-m72`. Its platform cannot be boarded on foot from the west bank with the DLL as it is (the rise is 32 px, the jump 27.5 px): the arc of (m2) has one teleport to the peninsula; whether the original reaches it otherwise is not established (question for the recipe).
- **O-G3-3 is pinned, not corrected** (D-E20-77): on the top of a crate the DLL sets the contact of the neighbouring crate one tick before the binary; a test names the DLL's value with the binary's in a comment (`CrateSolidityMap392Tests`); its cause is that outside the ground-walk branch of `ComputeXYPosition` (`0x80037730`) the binary returns the candidate of the penultimate attempt of its binary search of the step, where the engine mover advances to the contact (ADR-0045 of the engine); a faithful fix probably belongs to the engine and is a later slice; the engine is not changed here.
- **Erratum of the montage**: the T0 probe rig (`CarryRig`) gave the idle animation the acceleration byte 0, the real hero sheet has 1; with the real value the DLL makes the binary's half step at the release of the walk and the contact lines B, D, D2 equal the oracle. The apparent gap of the walk release was a defect of the rig, not of the game; `CarryRig` now sets the byte to 1 (no existing test moved).
- **Verification**: 24 real mutants (applied by script, undone, `docs/plan-e20-annexe/e20-g1b/t4/`), all killed; the one that first survived (the interaction before the lift) forced the test of an object both liftable and interactive. The contact of "the current tick" cannot be mutated by a line of the DLL (the order of the tick fixes it); the mutant that never clears the contact is killed instead.
- Revert: phase B alone (empty set, or the stub body, and the climb calls); phase A is pure code and tests.
